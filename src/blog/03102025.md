The phrase master's tool has become famous due to the essay The Master’s Tools Will Never Dismantle the Master’s House by Audre Lorde. Often this essay comes up when discussing technology and justice. What is a master's tool, and what differentiates the master's tool from simply a tool? I would argue that the phrase master's tool implies some sort of possession and ownership. In the hands of the master, the tool is turned towards some end. The master's tool is also in some ways fashioned or shaped for the master, it also has affordances that shape possible actions that the master takes. But what happens to the master's tool in the hands of the slave, invoking the idea of the master slave dialectic but switching emphasis to the slave.

Is Python a master tool? Is data analysis? Is a CSV file, is data? Is objectivity or math a master's tool? I would argue that what makes the master's tool the master's tool is that‘ it‘ the masters hand and deployed for the masters end. However the question of whether something is ontologically a masters tool or can be reapproproasted is contingent upon deeper investigation into the tool itself alongside the politics, values, and paradigm that guide its development, affordances, and deployment


> What makes a tool a master's tool?

Some tools might be a master's tool, because of their purpose, their affordances, and the material conditions under which it was developed. For example, Palantir is fundamentally a state surveillance software, and we have to be careful not to use tools like the master. Similarly, there has been much work to define the concept of platform capitalism. A platform, as it currently exists under capitalism, is an enclosed environment where users have access to various services, and data is constantly siphoned off from their activity and sold to make a profit. While these platforms allow people to connect, make content, and in many cases make money, this is merely one part of the purpose of these systems. These are a couple of examples of what could be considered master's tools.
